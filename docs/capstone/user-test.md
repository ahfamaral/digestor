# Digestor: user test journey

A moderated test of about 40 minutes. It shows whether a journalist can go from a long interview to a subtitled clip without help, and whether they trust what the AI writes.

## Who the tester is

A working journalist or a journalism student who has cut video or worked with interview transcripts. They do not need to know Digestor or any technical terms. One tester per session.

Language note: the interview is in German. A German-speaking tester can judge the German quotes and the subtitles. A Portuguese-speaking tester can use the Portuguese or English subtitle translation, and should say so before starting. If the tester speaks neither, they use the English translation and the notes say so in the results table.

## Setup (facilitator, before the tester arrives)

- Open https://digestor.freitasdoamaral.com in a fresh browser window on a laptop. Give the tester the password in person. Never write it in this document, in notes or in the recording.
- The Osang x Merkel interview is already analysed. Check that it appears on the Sessions page as ready, and that no clips exist yet from an earlier test (there are no accounts: delete earlier test clips on Prepare first).
- Prepare a stopwatch, the results table below, and the screen recording.
- Do not demonstrate anything. Do not hint at button names.

## Consent (read aloud, tester agrees before starting)

"We are testing the software, not you. With your agreement I will record the screen and your voice. I will take notes under a number, not your name. The recording and notes are used only for this project, and you can stop at any time or ask me to delete them."

Tester agrees: yes / no. Recording: yes / no.

## What to say at the start

"You are preparing a short piece about this interview. Please think aloud: say what you are looking at, what you expect and what confuses you. I will not be able to help, but I will take notes. There are no wrong answers."

Read each task aloud, one at a time. Do not use the words in brackets, they are only for the facilitator.

## Tasks

### Task 1. Find what Merkel said about Putin and keep it

"Find where Angela Merkel talks about Putin in this interview, and mark the parts you would want to use."

[Expected path: open the session from Sessions, read the Briefing page, find or search a topic, press Approve on one or more moments. Skip is the other choice.]

Success: at least one moment about Putin is Approved.

### Task 2. Check who is speaking

"Before you use a quote, make sure you know who says it. Check that the names are right."

[Expected path: Who's who, confirm or fix the names of the speakers.]

Success: the tester finds the speaker names and says whether they are correct.

### Task 3. Make a vertical clip with subtitles

"Turn your selection into a short clip for a phone screen, with subtitles in a language you can read."

[Expected path: Prepare clips, trim the clip if wanted, choose the 9:16 format, add subtitles in a language, approve the subtitles, set a credit line.]

Success: a clip in 9:16 with an approved subtitle track in one language.

### Task 4. Ask a question and check the answer

"Ask the tool a question about the interview that you would want answered before publishing. Then decide whether you believe the answer."

[Expected path: open Ask (the Ask button or the / key on the Briefing page), type a question. The tester should find the time stamp in the answer and open the video at that point.]

Success: the tester checks at least one time stamp against the video and says whether it matches.

### Task 5. Let Ask change something, and decide

"Ask the tool to do something for you in the clips, for example to make one clip shorter or to select another part of the interview. Look at what it proposes before it happens."

[Expected path: Ask shows an approval card with Approve, Edit and Reject before anything changes. The tester picks one. If the card does not appear, write that down and continue.]

Success: the tester understands what will change and chooses Approve, Edit or Reject on purpose.

### Task 6. Export

"Get your clip out of the tool so you could send it to an editor."

[Expected path: Export, then Download all or a single clip download. Burned subtitles, separate subtitle files or the plain clip.]

Success: a file is downloaded and the tester says what is in it.

## What to observe for each task

Write short notes, not interpretations. Do not help. If the tester is stuck for 3 minutes, say "Let's move on" and mark the task as not completed.

| Observe | How |
|---|---|
| Time | Seconds from reading the task to success or giving up |
| Hesitations | Pauses longer than 5 seconds, and where on the screen |
| Wrong clicks | Anything clicked that did not lead toward the task |
| Words used | What the tester calls things (moment, clip, subtitles, names) |
| Trust in AI text | Did they check the answer, a summary or a title against the video? What did they say about it? |
| Help needed | None / hint asked / stuck |
| Quotes | Exact sentences said aloud, in the tester's words |

## After the tasks: questionnaire

Tester answers alone on paper. Scale: 1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree.

1. I think I would use this tool often in my work.
2. I found the tool more complicated than it needed to be.
3. I found the tool easy to use.
4. I felt confident using the tool.
5. I would need a lot of learning before I could use this tool.
6. I felt I could rely on the text the AI wrote (titles, summaries, answers).

Scoring for the facilitator: for items 1, 3, 4 and 6 use the answer minus 1. For items 2 and 5 use 5 minus the answer. Add the six numbers and multiply by 100/24 for a score from 0 to 100. With six items and one tester this is only a rough indicator.

Open questions (read aloud, write the answer word for word):

1. What was the most difficult moment?
2. What did the tool do that you did not expect?
3. Tell me about the last time you worked with a long video or interview. How would this tool fit into that?
